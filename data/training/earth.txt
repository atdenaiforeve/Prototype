Earth is a planet in the Solar System.
Earth is the third planet from the Sun.
Earth has liquid water on much of its surface and an atmosphere that supports many forms of life.
Earth is approximately spherical, although its shape is slightly flattened at the poles and irregular because of its surface features.
Earth rotates around its axis.
One rotation of Earth takes about one day.
Earth orbits the Sun.
One orbit of Earth takes about one year.
Earth's axis is tilted relative to its orbit.
Earth's axial tilt contributes to the changing seasons as Earth travels around the Sun.
The atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding Earth.
Earth's atmosphere is mostly nitrogen and oxygen, with smaller amounts of other gases.
The atmosphere helps regulate temperature and provides gases needed by living organisms.
The atmosphere has layers with different properties.
The troposphere is the lowest major atmospheric layer and is where most weather occurs.
The stratosphere lies above the troposphere and contains the ozone layer.
The ozone layer absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.
Earth's surface includes oceans, continents, islands, mountains, plains, valleys, deserts, forests, and many other environments.
Oceans cover most of Earth's surface.
Earth's continents are large connected landmasses separated by oceans and other geographic features.
Earth has seven commonly recognized continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, South America, and Australia.
Earth has five commonly recognized oceans: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, and Arctic.
Australia is both a country and the name commonly used for one of Earth's continents.
The Pacific Ocean is the largest ocean.
The Arctic Ocean surrounds the Arctic region.
Antarctica is a continent surrounding the South Pole.
Earth has a layered internal structure.
The crust is Earth's thin outer rocky layer.
The mantle lies below the crust and is much thicker.
The outer core is a liquid layer mainly made of iron and nickel.
The inner core is a solid layer mainly made of iron and nickel.
Earth's internal heat contributes to movement in the mantle and geological activity.
Tectonic plates are large pieces of Earth's rigid outer shell that move slowly.
Plate tectonics describes the movement and interactions of tectonic plates.
Plates can move apart, move toward one another, or slide past each other.
Divergent boundaries occur where plates move apart.
Convergent boundaries occur where plates move toward each other.
Transform boundaries occur where plates slide past one another.
Earthquakes can happen when stress is released along faults or within Earth's crust.
Volcanoes can form where magma reaches the surface.
Magma is molten rock below Earth's surface.
Lava is molten rock that reaches Earth's surface.
Mountains can form through tectonic processes, volcanic activity, erosion, or combinations of these processes.
Erosion moves rock and sediment from one place to another.
Weathering breaks rocks down at Earth's surface.
Water, wind, ice, and living organisms can contribute to weathering and erosion.
Sediment is material such as rock fragments, mineral particles, or organic matter that can be transported and deposited.
Rivers can erode land, transport sediment, and create valleys and floodplains.
Glaciers are large masses of moving ice that can shape landscapes.
The water cycle describes the continuous movement of water through Earth's atmosphere, surface, and underground.
Evaporation changes liquid water into water vapor.
Condensation changes water vapor into liquid water.
Precipitation includes rain, snow, sleet, and hail.
Collection occurs when water gathers in oceans, lakes, rivers, soil, ice, or other reservoirs.
Groundwater is water stored below Earth's surface.
The hydrosphere includes Earth's water in oceans, ice, groundwater, lakes, rivers, and the atmosphere.
The biosphere includes the parts of Earth where life exists.
The geosphere includes Earth's solid rocky components.
The atmosphere, hydrosphere, geosphere, and biosphere interact as parts of the Earth system.
Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions.
Climate describes long-term patterns of weather in a region.
Sunlight provides most of the energy that drives Earth's climate and many surface processes.
The greenhouse effect helps keep Earth warm enough for liquid water and life.
Natural greenhouse gases include water vapor, carbon dioxide, and methane.
Earth's surface is constantly changed by geological, chemical, and biological processes.
Soils contain minerals, organic matter, air, water, and living organisms.
Soil supports many plants and is an important part of ecosystems and agriculture.
The carbon cycle moves carbon among the atmosphere, living organisms, soils, oceans, and rocks.
The nitrogen cycle moves nitrogen through the atmosphere, soil, water, and living organisms.
Earth has natural resources including water, minerals, soils, forests, and energy resources.
Renewable resources can be replenished naturally on human timescales, although they can still be overused.
Nonrenewable resources form over very long periods and can be depleted.
Human activities can change land, water, atmosphere, ecosystems, and climate.
Pollution can harm organisms and ecosystems.
Deforestation can remove habitats and change water and carbon cycles.
Conservation aims to protect ecosystems, species, natural resources, and Earth's processes.
Biodiversity is the variety of life on Earth.
Earth is home to an enormous variety of organisms and ecosystems.
Scientists study Earth using observations, experiments, measurements, maps, satellites, sensors, fieldwork, and computer models.
Geology studies Earth's solid materials, structure, history, and processes.
Oceanography studies oceans and their physical, chemical, geological, and biological systems.
Atmospheric science studies the atmosphere and processes such as weather and climate.
Earth science combines knowledge from many fields to understand the planet as a connected system.
