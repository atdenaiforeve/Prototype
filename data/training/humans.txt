Humans are living organisms and members of the species Homo sapiens.
Humans are animals and are vertebrates.
Humans are mammals because they have hair, produce milk for young, and have other mammalian characteristics.
Humans are primates and are closely related to other apes.
A human body is made of cells, tissues, organs, and organ systems.
A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of living organisms.
Human cells contain genetic information primarily in DNA.
DNA carries biological instructions used in development, maintenance, and reproduction.
Genes are sections of DNA that contribute to biological traits and cellular functions.
Chromosomes are structures containing DNA and associated proteins.
Most human cells contain 23 pairs of chromosomes.
Humans inherit genetic material from biological parents.
Genetic variation contributes to differences between individuals.
A mutation is a change in DNA sequence.
Not every mutation has a noticeable effect on a person's traits or health.
The human genome contains the genetic information of Homo sapiens.
The nucleus of many human cells contains chromosomes.
Mitochondria help cells release usable energy from nutrients.
Ribosomes build proteins using information carried by RNA.
The cell membrane controls movement of many substances into and out of a cell.
Human tissues include epithelial, connective, muscle, and nervous tissue.
Organs are structures made from multiple tissues that perform specific functions.
Organ systems are groups of organs that work together.
The skeletal system provides support, protection, and places for muscles to attach.
An adult human skeleton normally has 206 bones, although anatomical variation can occur.
The skull protects the brain.
The vertebral column supports the body and protects the spinal cord.
The rib cage helps protect the heart and lungs.
Joints are places where bones meet.
Ligaments connect bone to bone.
Tendons connect muscles to bones.
Cartilage provides flexible support and reduces friction at many joints.
Muscles produce force that helps the body move and maintain posture.
Skeletal muscles are generally under voluntary control.
Smooth muscle works automatically in organs such as the digestive tract and blood vessels.
Cardiac muscle forms the heart and contracts rhythmically.
The muscular and skeletal systems work together to produce movement.
The nervous system detects information, processes signals, and coordinates many body functions.
The brain is the main organ of the central nervous system.
The spinal cord carries signals between the brain and much of the body.
Nerves connect the central nervous system with the rest of the body.
Neurons are cells specialized for transmitting electrical and chemical signals.
The brain has many interconnected regions with different but overlapping functions.
The cerebrum is involved in perception, thought, language, memory, planning, and voluntary movement.
The cerebellum contributes to coordination, balance, and motor learning.
The brainstem connects major brain regions and helps regulate vital functions.
The autonomic nervous system regulates many involuntary body processes.
The senses provide information about the environment and the body's internal state.
Vision uses light-sensitive cells in the retina.
The eye focuses light onto the retina.
The optic nerve carries visual information toward the brain.
Hearing begins when sound waves cause structures in the ear to vibrate.
The inner ear contains structures involved in hearing and balance.
Smell depends on receptors that detect chemical molecules in the nasal cavity.
Taste uses receptors that detect chemicals in food and drink.
Touch includes sensations such as pressure, temperature, vibration, and pain.
Pain is a protective sensory signal that can indicate actual or potential tissue damage.
The endocrine system uses hormones to regulate many processes.
Hormones are chemical messengers released by cells or glands.
The pituitary gland helps regulate several other endocrine functions.
The thyroid gland produces hormones involved in regulating metabolism and development.
The adrenal glands produce hormones involved in stress responses and other functions.
The pancreas has endocrine functions and also produces digestive enzymes.
Insulin helps regulate blood glucose concentration.
The circulatory system transports oxygen, nutrients, hormones, carbon dioxide, and other substances.
The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood through the circulatory system.
The heart has four chambers in humans: two atria and two ventricles.
Arteries generally carry blood away from the heart.
Veins generally carry blood toward the heart.
Capillaries are tiny blood vessels where exchange occurs between blood and tissues.
Red blood cells transport much of the body's oxygen using hemoglobin.
White blood cells help defend the body against pathogens and abnormal cells.
Platelets help blood clot after blood vessels are damaged.
Blood plasma is the liquid component of blood.
The respiratory system brings oxygen into the body and removes carbon dioxide.
Air travels through the nose or mouth, airways, and into the lungs.
The trachea is a major airway leading toward the lungs.
The bronchi carry air into the lungs.
Alveoli are tiny air sacs where gases are exchanged between air and blood.
The diaphragm is an important muscle involved in breathing.
During normal inhalation, the diaphragm contracts and helps expand the chest cavity.
The digestive system breaks food into smaller molecules that can be absorbed or used.
Digestion begins in the mouth with chewing and chemical processes.
The esophagus carries swallowed food toward the stomach.
The stomach uses mechanical and chemical processes to help digest food.
The small intestine is a major site of digestion and nutrient absorption.
The large intestine absorbs water and processes remaining material.
The liver performs many functions including processing nutrients and producing bile.
The gallbladder stores and concentrates bile.
The pancreas produces digestive enzymes and hormones.
The kidneys filter blood and help regulate water, salts, acids, and waste products.
The urinary system removes certain waste products from the blood.
The immune system protects the body against pathogens and abnormal cells.
The skin forms an important protective barrier against the external environment.
The skin contains structures involved in sensation, temperature regulation, and protection.
Sweating can help cool the body when sweat evaporates from the skin.
The body's internal temperature is regulated within a relatively narrow range.
Homeostasis is the maintenance of stable internal conditions despite changes in the environment.
The immune system includes physical barriers, cells, tissues, organs, and chemical defenses.
Innate immunity provides rapid, general defenses.
Adaptive immunity develops targeted responses to particular antigens.
Antibodies are proteins produced by certain immune cells that can recognize specific targets.
Vaccination trains the immune system to respond more effectively to specific infectious agents.
A pathogen is an organism or infectious agent that can cause disease.
Viruses require host cells to reproduce.
Bacteria are single-celled organisms, and many are harmless or beneficial while some cause disease.
Fungi include organisms such as yeasts and molds, and some can cause infections.
Parasites live in or on hosts and obtain resources from them.
Antibiotics are medicines used against susceptible bacterial infections and do not treat viruses.
Nutrition provides energy and materials needed for growth, repair, and normal body functions.
Carbohydrates, fats, and proteins are major classes of nutrients that can provide energy or building materials.
Vitamins and minerals are required in smaller amounts for many biological processes.
Water is essential for human cells and body functions.
A balanced diet supplies a variety of nutrients in appropriate amounts.
Metabolism is the collection of chemical reactions occurring in cells and organisms.
Basal metabolic processes require energy even when a person is resting.
Physical activity increases energy use and affects muscles, the heart, lungs, and metabolism.
Sleep is a normal biological state important for physical and mental functioning.
The sleep-wake cycle is influenced by internal biological rhythms and environmental cues.
Memory is the ability to encode, store, and retrieve information.
Learning involves changes in knowledge, skills, behavior, or neural connections through experience and practice.
Emotions involve coordinated physiological and psychological processes.
Stress is a response to perceived demands or threats and can involve changes in many body systems.
Human behavior is influenced by biology, learning, environment, culture, relationships, and individual experience.
Human development occurs across the lifespan.
Infancy includes rapid physical and neurological development.
Childhood includes major changes in physical growth, learning, language, and social development.
Adolescence is a developmental period involving physical, neurological, emotional, and social changes.
Puberty is a biological process involving hormonal changes and sexual maturation.
Adulthood includes continued learning, adaptation, and changes across many stages of life.
Aging involves gradual biological changes over time and differs greatly between individuals.
Reproduction allows humans to produce offspring.
Human reproduction normally involves sperm and an egg combining genetic material.
Fertilization produces a zygote containing genetic material from both biological parents.
Embryonic and fetal development occur during pregnancy.
The placenta allows exchange of gases, nutrients, and waste products between the pregnant person's blood supply and the developing fetus without normally mixing the two blood supplies directly.
After birth, newborns require substantial care and nutrition.
Humans are social animals that commonly live in families, communities, and larger societies.
Communication can use speech, writing, gestures, facial expressions, signs, images, and other systems.
Language allows humans to communicate complex information and abstract ideas.
Culture includes shared knowledge, practices, values, beliefs, technologies, and traditions within groups.
Humans learn from other people through teaching, imitation, cooperation, and communication.
Cooperation allows groups to accomplish tasks that may be difficult for one individual.
Human societies develop institutions, laws, customs, economies, technologies, and systems of education.
Tools extend human abilities and have been used by humans for a very long time.
Humans can make and use complex tools, machines, computers, and other technologies.
Agriculture involves cultivating plants and managing animals for food and other resources.
Cities are large human settlements with dense populations and complex infrastructure.
Human history includes migrations, technological changes, cultural developments, conflicts, cooperation, and scientific discoveries.
Archaeology studies past human activity using physical evidence.
Anthropology studies humans and human cultures from biological, cultural, linguistic, and archaeological perspectives.
Human geography studies relationships between people, places, environments, and spatial patterns.
Science uses systematic observation, measurement, testing, evidence, and reasoning to investigate the natural world.
Humans use mathematics to describe quantities, patterns, structures, and relationships.
Engineering applies scientific and mathematical knowledge to design and build useful systems.
Human technology can produce major benefits but can also create environmental and social challenges.
Humans depend on ecosystems for resources and ecological services.
Human activities can alter habitats, biodiversity, climate, water systems, and nutrient cycles.
Conservation aims to protect biodiversity, ecosystems, and natural resources.
Different humans can have different physical traits while belonging to the same species.
Human biological variation is influenced by genetics, development, environment, nutrition, health, and many other factors.
A person's appearance does not reliably reveal their abilities, personality, intelligence, or worth.
Human bodies have common biological structures but also substantial individual variation.
Health is influenced by biological, environmental, behavioral, social, and economic factors.
Medicine uses scientific knowledge to prevent, diagnose, manage, and treat disease and injury.
Public health focuses on protecting and improving the health of populations.
Clean water, sanitation, vaccination, nutrition, medical care, and disease prevention can improve population health.
Humans can adapt behavior and technology to many different environments.
The human body is an interconnected system in which organs and processes affect one another.
Understanding humans requires combining biology, psychology, neuroscience, anthropology, history, and other fields.
Humans are one species within Earth's larger web of life.
