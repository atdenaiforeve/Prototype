Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space.
Matter is made from particles.
Atoms are basic units of ordinary matter.
An atom contains a nucleus surrounded by electrons.
The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.
Protons have positive electric charge.
Electrons have negative electric charge.
Neutrons have no net electric charge.
The number of protons in an atom determines which chemical element it is.
An element is a substance made of atoms with the same number of protons.
The periodic table organizes chemical elements by atomic number and recurring properties.
Hydrogen is the simplest element.
Oxygen is an element important to many biological and chemical processes.
Carbon is an element that forms the backbone of many molecules in living organisms.
Iron, copper, gold, silicon, and many other substances are elements.
Isotopes are forms of an element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
Atoms can gain or lose electrons and become ions.
A molecule is a group of atoms held together by chemical bonds.
A compound contains atoms of two or more different elements chemically bonded together.
Water is a compound made from hydrogen and oxygen.
Carbon dioxide is a compound made from carbon and oxygen.
Chemical bonds hold atoms together in molecules and compounds.
Ionic bonds involve attraction between oppositely charged ions.
Covalent bonds involve atoms sharing electrons.
Metallic bonding occurs in metals and involves shared electrons throughout a structure.
Matter commonly exists as solids, liquids, gases, and plasma.
A solid has a definite shape and volume under ordinary conditions.
A liquid has a definite volume but takes the shape of its container.
A gas has neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.
Plasma is an energized state of matter containing charged particles and is common in stars.
Particles in solids generally vibrate around relatively fixed positions.
Particles in liquids can move past one another while remaining relatively close together.
Particles in gases are generally much farther apart and move freely.
Temperature is related to the average kinetic energy of particles in a substance.
Heating a substance can increase particle motion.
Cooling a substance can decrease particle motion.
Melting changes a solid into a liquid.
Freezing changes a liquid into a solid.
Evaporation changes liquid into gas at a surface.
Boiling changes a liquid into gas throughout the liquid when suitable conditions are reached.
Condensation changes gas into liquid.
Sublimation changes a solid directly into a gas.
Deposition changes a gas directly into a solid.
Physical changes alter a substance's form without changing its chemical identity.
Chemical changes produce new substances with different chemical compositions.
Rusting is a chemical process involving iron and oxygen and often water.
Combustion is a chemical reaction that releases energy, often involving oxygen.
Chemical reactions rearrange atoms and change how they are bonded.
Reactants are substances present at the start of a chemical reaction.
Products are substances formed by a chemical reaction.
The law of conservation of mass states that mass is conserved in ordinary chemical reactions in a closed system.
Energy is the capacity to cause change or do work.
Energy can be transferred between objects or transformed from one form to another.
Kinetic energy is energy associated with motion.
Potential energy is stored energy associated with position, arrangement, or other conditions.
Gravitational potential energy depends on position in a gravitational field.
Elastic potential energy can be stored when an elastic object is stretched or compressed.
Thermal energy is associated with microscopic motion and interactions of particles.
Chemical energy is associated with chemical bonds and molecular arrangements.
Electrical energy involves electric charges and electric fields.
Radiant energy is carried by electromagnetic radiation such as light.
Nuclear energy is associated with changes in atomic nuclei.
Energy is conserved overall, although it can change form or move between systems.
Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or work is done.
The watt is a unit of power.
The joule is a unit of energy.
Temperature can be measured using scales such as Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin.
The Kelvin scale begins at absolute zero.
Absolute zero is the theoretical lowest possible temperature.
Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.
Thermal conduction transfers energy through direct interactions between particles.
Convection transfers thermal energy through the movement of fluids.
Radiation transfers energy through electromagnetic waves and does not require matter between the source and receiver.
Metals often conduct heat and electricity effectively.
Insulators resist the flow of heat or electric charge more strongly than conductors.
Electric current is the flow of electric charge.
Voltage is a difference in electric potential that can drive current.
Resistance describes opposition to electric current.
Ohm's law relates voltage, current, and resistance in many electrical circuits.
A circuit provides a path through which electric charge can move.
Batteries can provide electrical energy by chemical reactions.
Magnets produce magnetic fields.
Electric currents can produce magnetic fields.
Changing magnetic fields can induce electric currents.
Electricity and magnetism are connected aspects of electromagnetism.
Light is electromagnetic radiation.
Electromagnetic radiation can travel through a vacuum.
The electromagnetic spectrum includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.
Different wavelengths and frequencies correspond to different parts of the electromagnetic spectrum.
Visible light is only a small part of the electromagnetic spectrum.
Reflection occurs when waves or light bounce from a surface.
Refraction is the change in direction of a wave when it enters a different medium.
Diffraction is the spreading or bending of waves around obstacles or through openings.
Sound is a mechanical wave that requires a medium such as air, water, or a solid.
Sound generally travels faster through solids than through gases.
Frequency is the number of repeating cycles per unit time.
Hertz is the unit used for frequency.
Amplitude describes the size of an oscillation and can relate to the energy carried by a wave.
Wavelength is the distance between corresponding points on successive waves.
Force is an interaction that can change an object's motion.
Newton is the SI unit of force.
Newton's second law relates net force, mass, and acceleration.
Acceleration is a change in velocity over time.
Velocity describes speed with direction.
Mass measures an object's inertia and is related to the amount of matter in an object.
Weight is the force of gravity acting on an object.
Friction is a force that resists relative motion between surfaces or through fluids.
Gravity causes objects near Earth to accelerate toward Earth's center when not otherwise supported.
Momentum depends on an object's mass and velocity.
A simple machine can make it easier to apply force or change its direction.
Examples of simple machines include levers, pulleys, wheels and axles, inclined planes, wedges, and screws.
Work in physics is energy transferred when a force causes displacement in the direction of the force.
Machines can transfer force and energy, but real machines lose some useful energy, often as thermal energy.
Density is mass divided by volume.
Objects can float or sink depending on density and the forces acting on them.
Pressure is force distributed over an area.
Fluid pressure can change with depth.
Matter and energy are central concepts in physics, chemistry, Earth science, biology, and engineering.
Scientists study matter and energy using measurements, experiments, mathematical models, and observations.
