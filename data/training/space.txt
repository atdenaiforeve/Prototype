Space is the vast region beyond Earth's atmosphere.
The observable universe contains enormous numbers of galaxies.
A galaxy is a large system of stars, gas, dust, and other material held together by gravity.
The Milky Way is the galaxy that contains our Solar System.
The Solar System is the system of the Sun and the objects that orbit it.
The Sun is a star at the center of the Solar System.
The Sun contains most of the mass of the Solar System.
The Sun produces energy through nuclear fusion in its core.
Nuclear fusion combines light atomic nuclei into heavier nuclei and releases energy.
The Sun's energy provides most of the energy that powers Earth's surface systems.
A planet is a large body that orbits a star and meets the accepted criteria for its planetary classification.
The eight planets of our Solar System are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.
Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun.
Venus has a very thick atmosphere and is the hottest planet in the Solar System at its surface.
Earth has abundant surface liquid water and supports known life.
Mars is a rocky planet with a thin atmosphere and evidence of ancient water activity.
Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System.
Saturn is famous for its extensive ring system.
Uranus rotates with an extreme axial tilt and is an ice giant.
Neptune is the farthest recognized planet from the Sun.
The four inner planets are rocky planets.
Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants.
Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.
Dwarf planets are smaller bodies that orbit the Sun and meet some but not all of the criteria used to define a planet.
Pluto is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt.
The Kuiper Belt is a region beyond Neptune containing many icy bodies.
Asteroids are rocky or metallic objects that orbit the Sun.
Many asteroids are found in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
Comets contain ice, dust, and rocky material.
A comet can develop a glowing coma and tail when solar heating releases material near the Sun.
Meteoroids are relatively small objects traveling through space.
A meteor is the visible streak produced when a meteoroid enters an atmosphere and heats the surrounding gas.
A meteorite is a space rock that survives passage through an atmosphere and reaches the ground.
Moons are natural satellites that orbit planets, dwarf planets, or other bodies.
Earth's Moon is Earth's natural satellite.
The Moon's gravity contributes strongly to Earth's ocean tides.
The Moon rotates and orbits Earth at rates that keep nearly the same side facing Earth.
The phases of the Moon result from changing viewing angles of the illuminated half of the Moon.
A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes between Earth and the Sun from a suitable alignment.
A lunar eclipse occurs when Earth passes between the Sun and Moon from a suitable alignment.
Gravity is the attraction between objects with mass.
Gravity keeps planets in orbit around stars and moons in orbit around planets.
An orbit is the curved path of an object under the influence of gravity and its motion.
Objects in orbit are continuously falling around the body they orbit.
A light-year is a unit of distance equal to the distance light travels in one year.
A light-year is a distance unit, not a unit of time.
Light travels extremely quickly, but it still takes time to cross astronomical distances.
Looking far into space allows astronomers to see objects as they were in the past because their light takes time to travel.
A star is a massive object made mostly of hot plasma that produces energy through nuclear processes.
Stars form when gravity causes clouds of gas and dust to collapse.
A protostar is a developing star before sustained hydrogen fusion begins in its core.
The mass of a star strongly affects its temperature, brightness, lifetime, and eventual fate.
Stars can have different colors related to their surface temperatures.
Blue-white stars are generally hotter than red stars.
Stars spend much of their lives in a stage where hydrogen is fused into helium in their cores.
The Sun is currently in this long-lived main-sequence stage.
When stars use up core hydrogen, their structures can change and they can become giant or supergiant stars.
The final evolution of a star depends strongly on its mass.
Some stars end as white dwarfs.
A white dwarf is the dense remnant of a low- or intermediate-mass star.
A supernova is a powerful stellar explosion or explosive stellar event.
Some massive stars can leave behind neutron stars after supernova explosions.
A neutron star is an extremely dense stellar remnant made mostly of neutrons.
A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that beyond its event horizon nothing, including light, can escape.
The event horizon is a boundary around a black hole.
Black holes can have different masses and can form through several astrophysical processes.
Supermassive black holes are found at the centers of many galaxies.
Nebulae are clouds of gas and dust in space.
Nebulae can be places where stars form or can be remnants of dying stars.
Star clusters are groups of stars held together by gravity.
A constellation is a pattern of stars as seen from Earth and does not usually mean the stars are physically close to one another.
The zodiac is a set of constellations and sky regions associated with the apparent path of the Sun.
A galaxy can have billions or more stars.
Galaxies can have spiral, elliptical, irregular, and other structures.
The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy.
Galaxies can interact and merge through gravity.
The universe is expanding.
The expansion of the universe means that on large scales distant galaxies are generally moving farther apart as space expands.
Dark matter is a name for unseen matter inferred from gravitational effects.
Dark energy is a name for the component associated with the observed accelerated expansion of the universe.
Astronomy is the scientific study of objects and phenomena beyond Earth.
Astronomers use telescopes to collect electromagnetic radiation and study distant objects.
Different telescopes can observe different wavelengths such as radio, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays.
Space telescopes can observe wavelengths that Earth's atmosphere blocks or weakens.
Spectroscopy can reveal information about the composition, temperature, motion, and other properties of astronomical objects.
The Doppler effect can cause observed wavelengths to shift when an object and observer move relative to one another.
Redshift can indicate that light has been shifted toward longer wavelengths.
Space is not completely empty; it contains gas, dust, radiation, particles, magnetic fields, and other phenomena.
Human spacecraft can carry instruments, satellites, rovers, and people.
Artificial satellites orbit Earth or other bodies for communication, navigation, science, weather observation, and other purposes.
The International Space Station is a crewed laboratory in low Earth orbit.
Space probes can travel to and study distant planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and other objects.
Robotic rovers have explored the surfaces of Mars and other planetary bodies.
Astronauts are people trained to travel and work in space.
Microgravity changes how people and objects behave compared with Earth's surface.
Earth is protected from much harmful space radiation by its atmosphere and magnetic field.
The universe contains many different environments, and scientists continue to discover new objects and phenomena.
