Mathematics is the study of quantity, structure, space, patterns, relationships, and change.
Numbers are mathematical objects used to represent quantities and other ideas.
Natural numbers are commonly used for counting.
Whole numbers include zero and the counting numbers.
Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero.
Rational numbers can be written as a ratio of integers with a nonzero denominator.
Irrational numbers cannot be written as a ratio of integers.
Real numbers include rational and irrational numbers.
Complex numbers extend the real numbers using the imaginary unit.
The imaginary unit is written as i and satisfies i squared equals negative one.
Zero represents the absence of a quantity in many contexts.
Positive numbers are greater than zero.
Negative numbers are less than zero.
An absolute value describes a number's distance from zero on the number line.
Addition combines quantities.
Subtraction finds a difference or removes a quantity.
Multiplication can represent repeated addition or scaling.
Division separates a quantity into equal parts or finds how many groups fit into a quantity.
A factor is a number that divides another number exactly.
A multiple is a number obtained by multiplying a number by an integer.
A prime number has exactly two positive factors: one and itself.
A composite number has more than two positive factors.
The greatest common factor is the largest positive factor shared by two or more integers.
The least common multiple is the smallest positive multiple shared by two or more positive integers.
A fraction represents a quantity as one integer divided by another nonzero integer.
The numerator is the top part of a fraction.
The denominator is the bottom part of a fraction.
Equivalent fractions represent the same value.
A decimal is another way to represent many fractions and real numbers.
A percentage represents a ratio out of one hundred.
Ratios compare quantities.
A proportion states that two ratios are equal.
An exponent represents repeated multiplication of a base in many common cases.
A base is the number or expression being raised to a power.
A square root of a nonnegative number is a number whose square equals that number.
Order of operations is a convention for interpreting arithmetic expressions.
Parentheses or other grouping symbols can indicate which operations are performed together.
Variables are symbols used to represent unknown or changing quantities.
A constant is a quantity that does not change within a specified context.
An expression combines numbers, variables, and operations.
An equation states that two expressions are equal.
An inequality compares quantities using symbols such as less than or greater than.
Solving an equation means finding values that make the equation true.
The distributive property states that multiplication can be distributed over addition or subtraction.
The commutative property means the order can be changed for addition or multiplication without changing the result.
The associative property means grouping can be changed for addition or multiplication without changing the result.
The identity property describes operations involving values such as zero for addition and one for multiplication.
A linear equation can often be represented by a straight line when graphed in two dimensions.
The slope of a line measures its rate of change.
The y-intercept is where a graph crosses the y-axis.
A function maps each allowed input to exactly one output.
The input of a function is often represented by x.
The output is often represented by f of x.
A domain is the set of allowed inputs of a function.
A range is the set of outputs produced by a function.
A sequence is an ordered list of numbers or other objects.
An arithmetic sequence has a constant difference between consecutive terms.
A geometric sequence has a constant ratio between consecutive nonzero terms.
A pattern is a repeated or structured relationship that can be described or predicted.
Algebra uses symbols and rules to represent and solve relationships.
Geometry studies shapes, sizes, positions, distances, angles, and properties of space.
A point represents a location and has no size in idealized geometry.
A line extends infinitely in two directions.
A line segment has two endpoints.
A ray has one endpoint and extends infinitely in one direction.
Parallel lines remain the same distance apart in a plane and do not intersect.
Perpendicular lines meet at a right angle.
An angle is formed by two rays sharing an endpoint.
A right angle measures ninety degrees.
An acute angle is less than ninety degrees.
An obtuse angle is greater than ninety degrees but less than one hundred eighty degrees.
A straight angle measures one hundred eighty degrees.
A triangle has three sides and three angles.
The interior angles of a triangle sum to one hundred eighty degrees in Euclidean geometry.
An equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three equal angles.
An isosceles triangle has at least two equal sides.
A scalene triangle has three different side lengths.
A quadrilateral has four sides.
A rectangle has four right angles.
A square has four equal sides and four right angles.
A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel opposite sides.
A rhombus has four equal sides.
A trapezoid has at least one pair of parallel sides under common definitions.
A circle is the set of points in a plane at a fixed distance from a center.
The radius is the distance from the center of a circle to its edge.
The diameter is twice the radius.
Circumference is the distance around a circle.
Area measures the amount of two-dimensional surface inside a boundary.
The area of a rectangle is length multiplied by width.
The area of a triangle is one half multiplied by base and height.
The volume of a rectangular prism is length multiplied by width multiplied by height.
Volume measures the amount of three-dimensional space occupied by an object.
Surface area measures the total area of the outer surfaces of a three-dimensional object.
The Pythagorean theorem relates the side lengths of a right triangle.
For a right triangle, a squared plus b squared equals c squared when c is the hypotenuse.
Coordinates can describe positions using ordered numbers.
The Cartesian coordinate plane uses horizontal and vertical axes.
The x-axis is horizontal in the standard coordinate plane.
The y-axis is vertical in the standard coordinate plane.
The origin is the point with coordinates zero and zero.
Statistics is the study of collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and communicating data.
A data set is a collection of observations or measurements.
The mean is the sum of values divided by the number of values.
The median is the middle value when data are ordered, with the average of the two middle values for an even-sized set.
The mode is the most frequently occurring value.
The range of a data set is the difference between its maximum and minimum values.
Probability measures how likely an event is.
A probability of zero represents an impossible event in a given model.
A probability of one represents a certain event in a given model.
For many equally likely outcomes, probability can be calculated as favorable outcomes divided by total outcomes.
A sample is a subset of a larger population used to learn about that population.
Correlation describes an association between variables but does not by itself prove causation.
Graphs can represent numerical relationships and make patterns easier to see.
A bar chart compares categories.
A line graph can show change across an ordered variable such as time.
A scatter plot shows pairs of numerical observations and can reveal relationships.
A mathematical proof is a logical argument that establishes a statement from accepted definitions, axioms, and previously established results.
Logic studies valid reasoning and relationships between statements.
Mathematical models use mathematics to represent real systems and make predictions.
Units give numerical measurements meaning.
The metric system uses units such as metres, kilograms, seconds, litres, and degrees Celsius.
Scientific notation represents very large or very small numbers using powers of ten.
Estimation provides an approximate value when an exact value is unnecessary or unavailable.
Checking an answer can reveal calculation errors or unreasonable results.
