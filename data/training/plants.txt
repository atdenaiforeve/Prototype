Plants are living organisms that make up an important part of most ecosystems.
Plants need water, light, nutrients, and suitable conditions to grow.
Most plants make their own food through photosynthesis.
Photosynthesis uses light energy to help a plant make sugars from carbon dioxide and water.
Oxygen is released during photosynthesis.
Chlorophyll is a pigment that helps plants capture light energy.
Leaves are major sites of photosynthesis in many plants.
Roots anchor plants and absorb water and dissolved minerals from soil.
Stems support plants and transport water and other substances.
Xylem transports water and minerals from roots through the plant.
Phloem transports sugars and other organic substances through the plant.
Flowers are reproductive structures in flowering plants.
Fruits can help protect seeds and help with seed dispersal.
Seeds contain a young plant and stored resources or structures that help support early growth.
Germination is the process in which a seed begins to grow.
Plants can reproduce sexually by producing seeds.
Some plants can also reproduce asexually through structures such as runners, bulbs, tubers, or cuttings.
Flowering plants are called angiosperms.
Conifers and related plants are gymnosperms that produce seeds that are not enclosed in fruits.
Ferns reproduce using spores rather than seeds.
Mosses are small plants that reproduce using spores.
Trees, shrubs, grasses, herbs, vines, and many other forms of plant life exist.
Trees usually have a main woody stem or trunk.
Shrubs usually have multiple woody stems near the ground.
Grasses have narrow leaves and often grow from the base.
Cacti are plants adapted to dry environments.
Succulents can store water in thick leaves, stems, or roots.
Mangroves are plants adapted to coastal environments that can be flooded by seawater.
Plants live in forests, grasslands, deserts, wetlands, mountains, freshwater habitats, and many other environments.
Rainforests can contain enormous plant diversity.
Desert plants often have adaptations that reduce water loss or help store water.
Plants in cold environments can have adaptations that help them survive low temperatures.
Aquatic plants can live partly or completely in water.
Plants respond to environmental signals such as light, gravity, temperature, and water availability.
Phototropism is plant growth in response to light.
Gravitropism is plant growth in response to gravity.
Roots commonly grow downward while shoots commonly grow upward.
Plants can open or close structures such as stomata to regulate gas exchange and water loss.
Stomata are tiny openings, often found on leaves, that allow gases to move in and out.
Carbon dioxide enters leaves through stomata for photosynthesis.
Transpiration is the loss of water vapor from plant surfaces, especially leaves.
Water moves through plants partly because of processes involving transpiration and the properties of water.
Plants need mineral nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium.
Nitrogen is important for molecules including proteins and nucleic acids.
Plants obtain nutrients from soil, water, air, or other sources depending on the nutrient and plant.
Some plants have partnerships with fungi around their roots.
Mycorrhizal fungi can help plants obtain nutrients and water while receiving sugars from plants.
Legumes can form relationships with bacteria that help convert atmospheric nitrogen into forms plants can use.
Plants can defend themselves from herbivores and disease.
Some plants produce chemicals that discourage herbivores or reduce damage.
Thorns, spines, tough leaves, and irritating substances can provide protection.
Some plants can trap insects and other small organisms and digest them for nutrients.
Venus flytraps and pitcher plants are examples of carnivorous plants.
A plant's structure is often related to its environment and way of life.
Plant adaptations are inherited features or behaviors that help organisms survive and reproduce in particular environments.
Pollination transfers pollen to a part of a flower where fertilization can occur.
Pollinators can include bees, butterflies, moths, birds, bats, and other animals.
Wind can also carry pollen between plants.
Seed dispersal can happen through wind, water, animals, or mechanical release.
Seeds can have structures that help them travel by wind or attach to animals.
Plants are important producers in food webs because they convert energy from sunlight into chemical energy.
Herbivores obtain energy by eating plants.
Carnivores can obtain energy indirectly from plants by eating herbivores.
Decomposers break down dead organic material and return nutrients to ecosystems.
Plants take part in the cycling of carbon, oxygen, water, and nutrients.
Forests can store large amounts of carbon in vegetation and soils.
Plants provide food, shelter, oxygen, materials, and habitats for other organisms.
Humans use plants for food, timber, fibers, medicines, fuels, landscaping, and many other purposes.
Crops are plants grown for useful products such as food or fiber.
Selective breeding has changed many crop plants over generations.
Agriculture depends on healthy soils, water, suitable climates, and successful plant growth.
Plant diseases can be caused by organisms or environmental conditions.
Pests can damage crops and reduce yields.
Biodiversity includes the variety of plant species and genetic variation within species.
Habitat loss can reduce plant populations and biodiversity.
Invasive species can alter ecosystems and compete with native plants.
Conservation can protect plant species, habitats, and genetic diversity.
A plant is not an animal, but plants and animals depend on one another in ecosystems.
Plants provide energy to many food webs, while animals can pollinate flowers and disperse seeds.
Plants also interact with fungi, bacteria, and many other organisms.
