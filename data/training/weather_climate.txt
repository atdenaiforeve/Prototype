Weather describes the short-term state of the atmosphere at a particular place and time.
Climate describes long-term patterns and averages of weather in a region.
Weather can change over minutes, hours, or days.
Climate is usually studied over many years or longer periods.
The atmosphere is a mixture of gases surrounding Earth.
Air pressure is the force exerted by air over an area.
Air pressure generally decreases as altitude increases.
Temperature describes how hot or cold something is.
Humidity describes the amount of water vapor in the air.
Relative humidity compares the amount of water vapor in air with the amount it could hold at that temperature.
Dew point is the temperature to which air must be cooled for saturation to occur at constant pressure.
Clouds form when moist air cools enough for water vapor to condense or deposit into tiny particles.
Cloud droplets and ice crystals can form around microscopic particles called condensation nuclei or ice-nucleating particles.
Cirrus clouds are generally high, thin clouds made mostly of ice crystals.
Cumulus clouds often appear as puffy clouds with strong vertical development possible.
Stratus clouds form broad, layered cloud sheets.
Cumulonimbus clouds can produce thunderstorms, heavy rain, hail, strong winds, and sometimes tornadoes.
Precipitation is water that falls from clouds to Earth's surface.
Rain is liquid precipitation.
Snow consists of ice crystals that fall from clouds.
Sleet is a term used for different types of frozen precipitation depending on region; in some places it means small ice pellets.
Hail consists of layered balls or irregular pieces of ice produced in strong thunderstorms.
Fog is a cloud at or very near Earth's surface.
Wind is moving air.
Air moves because of differences in pressure, with other forces and Earth's rotation affecting its movement.
A pressure gradient is a change in pressure across distance and helps drive wind.
The Coriolis effect results from Earth's rotation and influences the direction of moving air and water on a rotating planet.
Sea breezes can develop because land and water heat at different rates.
Land breezes can develop when cooling patterns reverse the local temperature contrast.
Global atmospheric circulation moves heat and moisture around the planet.
The equator receives more direct average solar energy than the poles.
Unequal heating helps drive large-scale atmospheric circulation.
Jet streams are relatively narrow bands of strong winds high in the atmosphere.
The water cycle connects evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff, infiltration, and other water movements.
Evaporation transfers liquid water into the atmosphere as water vapor.
Transpiration transfers water vapor from plants into the atmosphere.
Evapotranspiration combines evaporation and transpiration.
Condensation changes water vapor into liquid water.
Precipitation returns water from the atmosphere to Earth's surface.
Runoff is water flowing over land toward streams, rivers, lakes, or oceans.
Infiltration is water entering soil or porous ground.
Weather fronts are boundaries between air masses with different properties.
A cold front occurs when colder air advances and can lift warmer air.
A warm front occurs when warmer air advances over colder air.
An occluded front can form when one front catches up with another.
A stationary front occurs when a boundary between air masses moves little.
Air masses are large bodies of air with relatively uniform temperature and moisture characteristics.
Thunderstorms form when warm, moist, unstable air rises and produces strong convection.
Lightning is a large electrical discharge in the atmosphere.
Thunder is the sound produced by rapid heating and expansion of air around lightning.
Severe thunderstorms can produce damaging winds, large hail, frequent lightning, and other hazards.
Tornadoes are rapidly rotating columns of air associated with some thunderstorms.
Tropical cyclones are large rotating storm systems that form over sufficiently warm ocean water under suitable conditions.
A tropical cyclone can be called a hurricane, typhoon, or cyclone depending on its region.
Tropical cyclones can bring strong winds, heavy rainfall, and dangerous coastal flooding.
Storm surge is an abnormal rise of seawater associated with a storm's winds and pressure.
Flooding occurs when water covers land that is normally dry.
Drought is an extended period of unusually low water availability relative to normal conditions.
Heatwaves are periods of unusually hot weather for a location and season.
Cold waves are periods of unusually cold weather for a location and season.
Bushfires and wildfires are influenced by weather, vegetation, fuel moisture, and human or natural ignition.
Climate is influenced by incoming solar energy, Earth's surface, atmosphere, oceans, ice, vegetation, and other factors.
The greenhouse effect is a natural process in which certain atmospheric gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation.
Greenhouse gases include carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, and water vapor.
Human activities have increased atmospheric concentrations of several long-lived greenhouse gases.
Increasing greenhouse gas concentrations can increase Earth's energy imbalance and warm the climate system.
Climate change can alter temperature patterns, precipitation, sea level, ice, ecosystems, and the frequency or intensity of some extremes.
Climate variability refers to natural changes in climate over different timescales.
El Niño and La Niña are phases of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation that can influence weather patterns around the world.
Ocean currents transport heat and influence regional climates.
The ocean stores and redistributes large amounts of heat.
Sea ice is frozen seawater, while glaciers are land-based ice.
Melting land ice contributes to sea-level rise.
Thermal expansion of seawater also contributes to sea-level rise as oceans warm.
Climate models use mathematical representations of physical processes to study and project climate behavior.
Weather forecasts use observations and computer models to predict likely atmospheric conditions.
Weather observations can come from ground stations, weather balloons, aircraft, ships, buoys, radar, and satellites.
Radar can help detect precipitation and track storms.
Satellites can observe clouds, atmospheric conditions, oceans, land, and weather systems.
Meteorology is the scientific study of the atmosphere and weather.
Climatology is the study of climate and its patterns and changes.
A forecast is a prediction about future weather conditions based on observations and models.
Forecast uncertainty exists because the atmosphere is complex and measurements and models have limitations.
The atmosphere is a chaotic system, so small differences in initial conditions can affect later weather predictions.
A weather warning communicates that dangerous conditions are occurring or expected.
Weather services use observations and forecasts to issue warnings for hazards.
Australia experiences many climate zones, from tropical conditions in the north to temperate and arid regions farther south and inland.
Western Australia includes tropical, arid, semi-arid, Mediterranean, and other climate influences across its large area.
Perth has a Mediterranean-type climate with hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters.
